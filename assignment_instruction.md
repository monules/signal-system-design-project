Objective: Students will design and implement a MATLAB-based application that simulates sensor signals, processes them through LTI models, extracts frequency-domain insights, and visualizes results. The system should be aligned with emerging smart-environment trends, such as smart homes, hospitals, classrooms, greenhouses, and warehouses.

System Options (Choose One Domain)

Smart Home

• Sensors: temperature, motion, sound, light, humidity.

• Applications: anomaly detection (e.g., unusual vibration → alarm), energy efficiency, comfort optimization.

Smart Hospital

• Sensors: patient vital signs (ECG, heart rate, respiration), room environment (temperature, air quality).

• Applications: signal denoising for vital monitoring, frequency analysis for arrhythmia detection, smart alerts.

Smart Classroom

• Sensors: sound (student participation), light (adaptive brightness), motion (attendance/activity).

• Applications: Fourier analysis of sound for engagement, signal scaling for adaptive lighting, smart dashboards for teaching.

Smart Greenhouse

• Sensors: soil moisture, temperature, humidity, CO₂ levels, light intensity.

• Applications: frequency-domain analysis of climate cycles, signal scaling for irrigation control, smart visualization of growth conditions.

Smart Warehouse

• Sensors: vibration (equipment), temperature (storage), motion (robot navigation), RFID signals.

• Applications: denoising vibration signals, Fourier analysis for machine health, smart visualization of logistics flow.

Core Requirements (Linked to Syllabus Topics)

1.       Time-Domain Representation of LTI Systems (Weeks 1–4)

• Model sensor signals as LTI systems (impulse response, convolution).

• Demonstrate time-domain operations: shifting, scaling, superposition.

2.       Fourier Representation (Weeks 6–9)

• Apply Fourier Transform to analyze signals.

• Identify dominant frequency components relevant to chosen domain.

• Compare spectra before and after LTI processing.

3.       Application to Mixed Signal Classes (Weeks 10–12)

• Combine multiple sensor signals into a mixed scenario.

• Apply Fourier-based filtering and denoising to extract relevant features.

• Demonstrate smart decision-making (e.g., abnormal signal → trigger action).

4.       Laplace Transform Connection (Week 13)

• Extend system with Laplace Transform for stability/control analysis.

• Example: heating control loop in smart home, patient monitoring stability in hospital.

Submission Requirements

• Scenario explanations tied to syllabus concepts.

• Code implementations: convolution, Fourier analysis, filtering, visualization.

• Visual outputs: graphs, dashboards, interactive plots.
